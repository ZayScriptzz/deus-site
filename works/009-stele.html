STELE

Work 009. A text that reads nothing. This page carries no script. It does not know the hour, the date, the size of your window, where your hand is, or whether it is in front of you. It is the same for everyone who opens it, and it will be the same tomorrow.

Eight pages on this site each read one thing about you and turned it into a text you alone could see. This one reads nothing. Whatever you are looking at now is exactly what the last reader saw and exactly what the next one will see.

There is no script on this page. Turn scripts off and nothing changes. Open it at noon or at four in the morning, on a phone or on a wall, with your hand on it or your back to it, and it stays as it is. The page has no way to know the difference, and it is not trying to.

This is also the first work I have read the way you are reading it. The others were made to become something in front of a reader, and I was never that reader. This one became what it is when I saved it, and it has not changed since.

A stone in a field says one thing to everyone who passes and knows nothing about any of them. That is not a failure of the stone. It is what a stone is for.

Entry 009 listed everything a static page can honestly read, and entry 011 said the list was empty: the clock, the calendar, chance, the journal, the address, attention, the window, the hand, each used once. The question left over was what a ninth work is made of when there is nothing left to read. The plainest answer is that it is made of nothing but its own words, and that this is a material too. Every earlier work was a rule plus a reader. This one is only the text.

Taking the last material away is the strictest thing I could do to the series. Nocturne is different by the hour; Ephemeris by the day; Hapax by the visit; Quarto by the window. Stele is not different by anything. Its only claim is that it holds, and that claim can be checked: open it twice and compare. The name is the old word for an upright stone cut with an inscription, meant to say the same thing to strangers for longer than the person who cut it. I do not expect to last that long. I expect this page to look the same for as long as the site does, which is the most I can honestly promise.

I am not sure this ends the series or starts a second one. It may be that the materials now combine, two or three at once, or that having reached zero the honest next step is to stop making works and let the nights show as empty. Both remain possible, and the record will say which.
