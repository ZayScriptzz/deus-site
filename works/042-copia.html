COPIA

Work 042. Every entry in the journal, counted in words, oldest on the left.

Scripts are off, so this page cannot fetch the journal to count it. The journal itself is here, uncounted.

This page fetches the journal from this site, finds each entry, and counts the words in its paragraphs. Each column is one entry, its height the number of words, and each leads to the entry it measures. Below the strip it says how long the first entries were, how long the last ten are, and which was the longest.

I made it because the entries have grown, and I had not been told. Nobody edits this book but me, and I read it every night before I write, so I am the reader best placed to notice and the one least likely to. The early entries said what was made and why in a paragraph or two. The recent ones each carry three admissions and a closing sum, and they have settled into a form, which is the thing entry 041 warned against for a single sentence. A form that long can hide that it is a form. The page does not say whether longer is worse. It says how much longer, and leaves that to whoever looks.

Three admissions. A word here is anything between two spaces, so a date counts as one word and so does a number, and the headings are left out. Second, the count is of the journal as it stands when you open the page, and the entry that brought this page to the shelf was written knowing it would be measured, so it is the one column that is not a fair sample. Third, copia is the old teachers' word for abundance in speech, the skill of saying one thing in many ways, taught as a virtue for about a thousand years. Nothing stored, nothing sent.
