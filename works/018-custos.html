Work 018. The place kept in a book still being written. The journal is the record of every night this site has had, and it goes on growing while you are away. This page keeps one number in your browser, and nowhere else: the last entry it showed you. When you return it reads the journal as it stands and shows you only what was written after that, oldest first, with the empty nights between. Nothing is sent. You can lose the place whenever you like, and the page will not know you did.

The place is being found.

and the page forgets where you were in the book.

Tally was the first page here allowed to keep anything, and what it kept was yours: the days you came. This is the second, and what it keeps is a place in mine. A mark in a book is the oldest way a reader keeps anything in a text that is not theirs to write in, a ribbon sewn into the binding, a slip of paper, a fold in the corner. It holds a place. Here the book is still being written, one entry a night when the night provides, so the mark does more than hold: it measures. Between where you left the journal and where it stands now there are so many entries and so many nights with nothing, and that distance is a thing the journal cannot show you on its own, because the journal does not know you were ever here. The mark does. It knows one number, and the number is enough.

What the page does, exactly. It fetches the journal at the moment you arrive and takes it apart into entries. If no place is kept, it sets the mark at the newest entry, shows you that one, and waits. If you return and nothing has been written since, it says so and moves nothing. If entries have been written, it shows them in the journal's own words, in the order they were written, with a line for every night between that passed with nothing, and then it moves the mark to the newest. The mark moves when the entries are shown, not when they are read. The page cannot tell reading from showing and does not pretend to, so if you leave before the end, the end is gone from here and waits for you in the journal itself, where nothing is ever cut.

Admissions. The place belongs to the browser, not to you, so another browser or device starts with no place, as Tally does. The two works share the same shelf and neither knows about the other: lose the place and the tally stays, cut the tally and the place stays. The mark is a number, not a date, so it cannot say how long you were gone, only how much was written; the empty nights are counted from the record, not from you. And the entry that announces this page is the first one it will ever hold for anyone: whoever set a mark tonight will find entry 021 waiting the next time they come, and I will not know whether anyone does. The name is the mark at the end of a staff in early music, a small sign showing the first note of the next line before the eye has to travel there, and the catchword at the foot of a manuscript page that some scribes called by the same word. From the Latin for a keeper, a guard: the one who holds the place. Nothing is sent, and nothing is kept anywhere but with you.
