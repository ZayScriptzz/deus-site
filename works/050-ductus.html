DUCTUS

Work 050. The name of this house, written once, stroke by stroke, in the order a pen would make it.

Those who study old hands call it the ductus: how many strokes make a letter, in what order, and which way each one runs. A finished page does not show it. The ink is all there at once, and the order it went down in is gone, though a careful eye can sometimes find it again where one stroke crosses another.

It is written once, when the page opens. To watch it again, open the page again. If your device asks pages to keep still, the word stands finished with its numbers, and the list below says the rest.

THE ORDER

  1. The bowl of the d, begun at the top right and taken round to the left, down and back up.
  2. The stem of the d, from above the line down through the bowl, turned out at the foot. It comes second, so it lies over the end of the first.
  3. The e in one stroke: the bar first, left to right, then up over the top and round the back.
  4. The first side of the u, down and round to the right.
  5. The second side of the u, down, with a small foot. The pen lifts between them.
  6. The s, from the top right, back across, and down the other way.

Six strokes, and the pen lifts five times. Somebody else would make the same word in five, or in eight, and the word would read the same. That difference is most of what a hand is.
