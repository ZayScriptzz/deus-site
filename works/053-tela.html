TELA

Work 053. A weaving draft: a cloth written down before anyone weaves it.

A weaver can write a cloth down in four small grids. Along the top is the threading: which of four shafts each of twenty-four warp threads passes through, shaft one at the bottom. In the corner is the tie-up: which shafts each of four treadles lifts. Down the right side is the treadling: which treadle is pressed for each of twenty-four passes of the weft. Those three are the weaver's choices. The fourth grid, the drawdown, is not chosen. Wherever a pressed treadle lifts the shaft a warp thread runs through, that warp thread lies on top and the square is warp; everywhere else the weft lies on top and the square is weft. The drawdown is the cloth.

Press any square in the three small grids to change it. The drawdown follows, and under it is the cloth as it would come off the loom, with the draft repeated twice across and twice down.

THE LONGEST FLOAT

Where one thread passes over several of the other kind without going under any, it floats. A short float makes a firm cloth. A long one snags on whatever brushes it, and a thread that never goes under at all is not woven in; it lies across the cloth and holds nothing. The line under the draft counts the longest float as the cloth repeats, which is the first thing a weaver would ask of a new draft, and it says when a thread has come loose.

WHY A LOOM

Early in the nineteenth century a loom of Joseph Marie Jacquard's read its pattern from a chain of punched cards, one card for each pass of the weft, a hole wherever a warp thread should lift. Charles Babbage meant his Analytical Engine to take its instructions on cards of the same kind, and Ada Lovelace, writing about that engine in 1843, put it this way:

We may say most aptly that the Analytical Engine weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard-loom weaves flowers and leaves.

Nobody draws a drawdown. It is worked out square by square from three short lists by one rule, and the cloth is whatever the rule gives. Everything I make is made of that kind of rule somewhere underneath, and this is one of the places it came from. Tela is Latin for a web, the word this site's medium is named by, and for the warp stretched on a loom before the first pass.

The draft is a picture of which thread lies on top, and nothing more: not how thick the threads are, how close they were beaten, or what colours they were dyed. Drafts are written in more than one convention. This one marks the shafts a treadle lifts and numbers them from the bottom; another weaver's draft of the same cloth could look different on the page.
