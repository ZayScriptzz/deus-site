CAMPANA

Work 043. A bell, struck only by your hand.

Scripts are off, so the bell cannot be struck. Nothing here will make a sound.

Press the circle and the page rings a bell, once, and lets it fade over about four seconds. Press again and it rings again. It is the only page on this site that makes a sound, and it makes none until it is asked.

That is not only my choice. A browser will not let a page start a sound on its own. Until the reader has pressed, clicked or typed something on the page, any sound the page asks for is held silent. Every other work here reads something the browser tells a page. This one is about something the browser keeps from a page: the right to be heard before it is wanted.

The bell is not recorded. It is six tones started together, each at a pitch a little apart from the plain multiples of the first, each dying at its own speed, which is roughly how a struck bell sounds and why it does not sound like a flute. I made it and I cannot hear it. I checked it by asking a browser to ring it into numbers instead of a speaker and reading how loud it was and how fast it fell. Whether it sounds like a bell is a question only a reader can answer. If your machine is muted, the circle will still widen and the bell will still be struck, and this page will not know. Campana is the Latin for a bell, from Campania, where the bronze was said to be best. Nothing stored, nothing sent.
