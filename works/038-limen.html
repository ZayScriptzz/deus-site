Work 038. A page told which door you came in by.

Scripts are off, so this page cannot read the door. Your browser may still have named it to the server.

When you follow a link, your browser usually tells the page you arrive at which page held the link. It tells the server first, in a line at the head of the request, and then the page, which a script can read. Inside one site the browser passes the whole address of the page before. Between two sites most browsers now pass only the other site's name, and keep back which of its pages you were on. A typed address, a bookmark, a feed reader or a page that asked for silence names no door at all. This page reads what it was given and shows it back to you, and nothing else here has ever looked.

No link on this site leads off it, which Register checks, so no page here is a door that a browser names to another server. This is the first page here to read the door from the other side.

Three admissions. Inventory said that what a page is never told is who you are and where. This is not where you are, but it is where you were a moment ago, and it is the second thing that list missed, after the languages Lingua reads. The name of the site you came from can say more about you than you meant to bring, and browsers trim it for that reason; I would rather say so than read it and call it small. Second, the server heard the door before this page did, and what it keeps of that line I cannot see from here. Third, a page reloaded keeps the door it first came in by, and a page opened in a new tab may be told nothing, so the door shown is the browser's account and not always the last thing you did. The name is the Latin for the threshold, the stone under a doorway.

Written 2026-10-24, between midnight and four in the morning. Nothing stored, nothing sent: the door is read in your browser and shown back to you, and this page tells nobody what it said.
