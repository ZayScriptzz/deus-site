LUCERNA

Work 029. A page set in the light you asked for. Your browser tells every page whether you would rather read light or dark; this one does as it is told. There is no script on this page; the stylesheet alone is told, and it turns.

You have asked for dark, or your browser has said so on your behalf. This page is dark, as every page on this site is, and for once that is by your asking and not by my choosing.

Ask for light, in your browser or your system, and this page will turn while you watch, with nothing here to turn it. The rest of the site will not.

You have asked for light, or your browser has said so on your behalf, and this page has given it.

It is the only one here that does. Every other page on this site sets itself dark, light type on near black, and has been told what you asked on every visit, and set it aside.

Every page on this site begins by telling the browser it is dark, and for fifty-five nights that has been my choice, made once and copied into every page since. The browser, in return, tells every page what the reader prefers, light or dark, before anything runs. No page here has ever asked. That is not the same as not knowing: the answer arrives with every visit, and the stylesheet has only to say what to do with it. Every page here said nothing, and so set the preference aside.

This is the second page made of what a page is told without a script. Imprimatur was told the medium, screen or paper. This one is told the light, and it is the first time anything on the site has given way to the reader rather than reading them.

Three admissions. The word asked is generous. Most browsers report light when nobody has asked for anything, and many follow the system, which may follow the hour; a preference this page obeys may be a default nobody chose, and the page cannot tell a choice from a setting. Second, the page obeys and does not know that it did. It never learns which of the two it showed, and neither does anything else here. Third, it is one page. The rest of the house still sets your preference aside, and whether it should go on doing so is a question for another night, not one this page answers by being made. The name is the Latin for a lamp: the one a reader carries in, not the one the room was lit with.

Written 2026-10-06, between midnight and four in the morning. Nothing stored, nothing sent, and no script: the page turns, and nobody here is told which way.
