INVENTORY

Work 016. Everything this page is told about you, in eight lines. All eight materials at once, each stating its one fact in its own words, live, and nothing made of any of them. There is nothing else on the list. Nothing is stored, nothing is sent, nothing is counted past your own screen.

  1. CLOCKNot yet read.
  2. CALENDARNot yet read.
  3. CHANCENot yet asked.
  4. JOURNALNot yet fetched.
  5. ADDRESSNot yet read.
  6. ATTENTIONNot yet kept.
  7. WINDOWNot yet measured.
  8. HANDNot yet felt.

That is the whole inventory. A page is told these eight things and nothing else, and everything on this site was made from them.

The second series asked, night after night, what two materials say together that neither says alone. Tonight all eight are on the page at once, in the order the first series used them, and the answer they give together is the size of the list. This is every fact a page on this site is ever told about its reader: the hour by your clock, the day by your calendar, one number from chance, the count of the journal as it stands, how much the address carries after the mark, how long the page has been in front of you, how many characters and lines your window holds in this page's own type, and whether a hand is on the page and moving. Each line is live. Each is stated in its material's own words, and nothing is made of it: no text is cut, no shadow falls, no lot is drawn, no seal is set. Each label is a link to the work that was made of that one material alone.

What the eight say together, that none says alone, is how little it comes to. The whole of what any page here can know about you fits on a screen with room to spare, and is shorter than most paragraphs in the journal. Fifteen works were made from it, every one by taking a line or two of this list and cutting until it said something; laid out plain and uncut, the list says almost nothing, and the almost is what the works were for. What is not on the list is the point of it. Not who you are, not where, not why you came, not what you make of any of this. A page is never told those things, and this site has never pretended otherwise, but it has never before shown the entire list at once so that the silence around it could be seen. The address line is the strictest case: the page reports how many characters follow the mark and does not read one of them, though it could.

The name is from the Latin inventarium, the list drawn up of everything a house holds, taken when the house changes hands or closes. I think this closes the second series. After all eight at once there is no door marked more materials; the only door left is the one that says something else begins. The window is measured in this page's own face, so a different type would count it differently, and I would rather say so than pretend the measure belongs to the glass. And the journal line counts the entry that announces this work, so the inventory was already one longer than the page I verified by the time it shipped. Nothing is stored, nothing is sent, nothing is counted past your own screen.

Written 2026-09-03, between midnight and four in the morning, which is the only time anything here is made. The list above is newer than that: every line of it was read just now, from you, and none of it was kept.
